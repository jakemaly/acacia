# Acacia execution research

Acacia should choose the next objective, a tactical approach, and the conditions that would change it. The bot should keep executing that objective across ordinary routing, collection, and survival events. Existing Minecraft bots demonstrate several parts of this split.

## Experiments for Acacia

1. **Make tool requests unambiguous.** The bridge trace shows why a build request should describe either a new portal or a block list. A chest deposit should expose `items` without the organize-only `item_order`. In this run fifteen deposit results rejected the same extra `item_order: []` before reaching chest I/O. Test a real chest with fixture items and verify both inventory loss and the chest gain. For older callers, an explicit empty arrange list on a deposit can be ignored if compatibility warrants it. Preserve rejection of a substantive conflicting request. [Chest trace](/home/jake/.local/share/mine-ai/e45782d3fc26/2026-11-13T23-17-19-997Z_01a1d733-83fc-7527-969e-aef884934b09.jsonl:581) · [Mine AI container contract](https://github.com/aibengineering/mine-ai-mcp/blob/de4fef3260424d973bf81d0eaa15ff1a2f73ecff/src/actions/use-container/contract.ts)
2. **Batch by intent.** Mine AI already has `collect_block` with coordinates optional and a requested count up to 32, and `collect_mob_drop`, which works toward a named drop quantity through approaching, combat, and pickup. A bounded area or connected-vein selector would let Acacia anchor to one deposit while retaining batching. Gravel needs separate controls for the source block, the wanted drop, and the maximum dig count. `Collect four flint from this patch, stop after 24 gravel blocks` leaves the RNG and excess-drop handling to the executor. Stop conditions should cover an exhausted patch, inventory capacity, a tool breaking, and a hazard that prevents continuing. [Block collection contract](https://github.com/aibengineering/mine-ai-mcp/blob/de4fef3260424d973bf81d0eaa15ff1a2f73ecff/src/actions/collect-block/contract.ts) · [Mob collection contract](https://github.com/aibengineering/mine-ai-mcp/blob/de4fef3260424d973bf81d0eaa15ff1a2f73ecff/src/actions/hunt-mob/contract.ts)
3. **Delegate route feasibility.** Ask for one route toward the fortress with allowances for safe digging or supported bridging and an explicit block budget. Stop at an actionable blocker such as absent verified landing, unsupported stairs, depleted scaffolds, or an exposure the executor cannot handle. Add candidate approach, closest reachable position, and obstruction evidence where they help decide the next move. The executor should try nearby equivalent landings while the model’s goal holds. An agreed restriction on lava drops, exposed bridging, or construction material can be evaluated as part of the path. [Baritone placement cost and replanning settings](https://github.com/cabaletta/baritone/blob/1.21.4/src/api/java/baritone/api/Settings.java)
4. **Measure observations before adopting full diffs.** Record model latency, new and cached input, output, and tool body size by source. Use a compact status with mandatory position, dimension, health, active action, and threat or inventory changes, then make the full report available. A true delta needs a baseline revision plus a resync on session restart, compaction, or unknown revision. Threshold cosmetic position and timer changes so a poll that says nothing new stays short. Keep full structured evidence on disk and provide drill-down for errors. Cache reads and new input are different measures, so raw transcript size does not establish prompt cost or the source of waiting time. [OpenAI cached input accounting](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)
5. **Evaluate the existing fight controller before replacing it.** Mine AI has blaze charge metadata, incoming projectile prediction, shield readiness, bow windows, and a guarded melee path. Some guards are deliberate. Its melee code keeps the shield raised around certain swings, and release forecasting reuses bow timing in parts of shield-facing code. Run repeatable skeleton, single-blaze, multiple-blaze, and constrained-footing fixtures with confirmed hit, damage, shield, cover, recovery, and pickup telemetry. Distinguish aim errors, shield raised before a shot is relevant, staying guarded after the volley, and action handoff delays. Measure useful attacks between volleys and damage per verified drop. [Threat perception](https://github.com/aibengineering/mine-ai-mcp/blob/de4fef3260424d973bf81d0eaa15ff1a2f73ecff/src/survival/perception/combat/observations.ts) · [Shield facing](https://github.com/aibengineering/mine-ai-mcp/blob/de4fef3260424d973bf81d0eaa15ff1a2f73ecff/src/survival/weapons/shield-facing.ts) · [Melee turns](https://github.com/aibengineering/mine-ai-mcp/blob/de4fef3260424d973bf81d0eaa15ff1a2f73ecff/src/survival/responses/fight/melee-turn.ts)

## When the model should get another turn

Mine AI already has a foreground action owner, safety interrupts, progress snapshots and a final-result gate. Build on that lifecycle. Acacia chooses a target, an approach, and acceptable limits. The foreground action owns searching, movement, equipment, pickup and recovery within those limits. An authorized defensive maneuver pauses the action, finds nearby cover, heals as needed and resumes from the current position. Return control on success, a measured stall, a meaningful change in threats, or a depleted budget. A retreat all the way to the portal is an expedition decision when local recovery and available alternatives cannot make the route viable. [Mine AI action lifecycle](https://github.com/aibengineering/mine-ai-mcp/blob/de4fef3260424d973bf81d0eaa15ff1a2f73ecff/docs/mcp/async-actions.md)

Treat the current 15-second Hardcore wait as a harness check interval. Let Pi check progress or retrieve the final result internally while the action remains productive, and ask the model when there is a decision to make. A prospective next action can be planned while the bot works. Its prerequisites need fresh validation at handoff because an unrelated mob, changed footing or depleted gear can invalidate it. Keep Stop and cancel responsive within the same body-ownership rules. [Current timeout handling](../src/pi.mjs)

For combat, the objective can name a protected approach, resource quantity, and maximum exposure. The executor observes attack windup, predicted projectile arrival, line of sight, shield readiness, reach, and melee cooldown. Let the loop block or take cover when needed, strike during verified openings, and reuse a protected position until it exhausts the approach. A compact result might say “3 of 8 rods collected, shield broken, covered fallback 6 blocks away.” Acacia then decides whether to swap gear, choose a new angle, or end the hunt. This keeps per-tick reactions below the model call and preserves its control of tactics.

The first blaze contact in the same run gave a separate example of an actual retreat. Opening the slit triggered defense. The controller recorded two attack attempts, an obstructed bow trajectory, zero registered projectile guards, and zero kills. A subsequent evade reached the covered bridge, ending about 40 blocks from the combat position with health still at 20/20. The trace supports testing line-of-fire selection and the distance chosen for cover before tuning shield timing. Later fights may expose distinct issues. ## How to compare versions

Use the same route, collection site, or combat arena for baseline and candidate, with multiple layouts and at least one lighting or threat change. A handful of similar scenarios helps distinguish a general primitive improvement from an exception that only works on this run’s seed. Measure model decisions per objective, time between tool completion and the next actionable work, actual movement and interaction time, repeated invalid calls, damage, drops gained, and blocks spent. In full runs track time and deaths at progression milestones. A higher percentage of moving ticks only matters when it comes with comparable survival and more progress.
